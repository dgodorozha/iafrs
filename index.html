HHRD

Why the question is open

Supervisors are building surveillance tools and circuit breakers for a market in which AI agents trade. The way those tools are validated can certify the one that fails at the decision it exists to inform.

Herding is plausible, and flagged

Trading agents built on shared foundation models can react in correlated ways, so that a routine shock becomes a dislocation. The Financial Stability Board, IOSCO, the Bank of England and the IMF have each raised the concern, and algorithmic trading already accounts for around 70 per cent of United States equity trading.

The yardstick is wrong

Surveillance tools and intervention rules are validated on aggregate fidelity: how well a synthetic market reproduces volatility, spreads and correlation. A tool can match those statistics and still fail at the supervisory decision, which agents are destabilising and when to act.

The record is empty

Historical back tests contain almost no episodes of machine herding, so a tool can only be scored on the decision in a generated market with a known truth. Public agent based studies describe behaviour; they benchmark no tools or rules and leave no audit trail on which a certification could rest.

How the results are read
PPRG

Programmes

Five lines of work, one question: when the traders are machines, how should a supervisor decide which tools and rules to trust, and how can that decision be certified?

CCRT

Decision first certification

Scoring surveillance tools on the supervisory decision rather than on resemblance to market statistics, with finite sample guarantees: conformal false alert bounds, winner stability and walk forward regret, so that the procedure, not one tool, is what is certified.

HHRD

Agentic markets and herding

Synthetic markets with a known truth: noise traders, fundamentalists, vendor clusters, reinforcement learning traders that learn to herd, distilled language model personas and colluding manipulators, and the dose response of concentration on a single model vendor.

IINT

Intervention rules

Circuit breakers, price bands, venue halts, dynamic throttles and kill switches across EU, UK and US style instruments, each scored on containment, false halts, tail risk, time to dislocation and proportionality against the untreated market.

GGOV

Governance, audit and assurance

The controls that let a supervisory system rest on generated evidence: human gates whose approver is checked against a register, signed replayable ledgers, critics that recompute every figure, transparency records and a red team that attacks the certified tool.

OOPN

An open benchmark

in preparation

A public library of scenario families and agent templates, written in a bounded declarative language and hashed into every battery that uses them, so that any authority can calibrate the markets to its own data, add its own families and compare its tools against the same truth as everyone else.

Independent evidence needs independent support.

Gifts and grants fund the open benchmark, independent audits of the certification procedure and the researchers’ time. Authorities can partner with the Institute to run a pilot on their own data.
